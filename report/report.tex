\begin{abstract}
This report forecasts monthly median dwelling prices in Porto (EUR/m$^2$) using multiplicative smoothing and decomposition methods, together with an ARIMA model fitted on a log-transformed, differenced series. The sample spans January 2011--February 2026 ($n=182$). A hold-out test set of 24 months (March 2024--February 2026) is used to compare methods. ETS achieves the lowest test RMSE (326~EUR/m$^2$); all methods under-forecast the accelerated growth in the test period. Out-of-sample 95\% prediction intervals for the next 24 months are reported for each approach.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and data description}

\subsection{Objective}

The objective is to forecast a real monthly time series using smoothing, decomposition, and ARIMA/SARIMA methods, to compare their predictive performance on a test set, and to produce out-of-sample forecasts with 95\% prediction intervals.

\subsection{Data source}

The variable is the monthly median price per square metre (EUR/m$^2$) for dwellings in Porto (series code \texttt{11A1312: Porto}). Data are stored in \texttt{timeSeriesPorto.csv} and cover January 2011--February 2026.

\subsection{Features of the series}

Figure~\ref{fig:time} shows a long upward trend: prices remain near 1\,000~EUR/m$^2$ until about 2015, then increase steadily with faster growth after 2020. A mild seasonal pattern is visible; the seasonal boxplot (Figure~\ref{fig:seasonal}) confirms that within-year variation is small relative to the trend. A spike around 2022--2023 is followed by a correction and renewed growth.

The ACF and PACF of the levels (Figure~\ref{fig:correlograms}) decay slowly with spikes at lags 12, 24 and 36, indicating non-stationarity and annual seasonality. Following the course guidelines, unit-root tests designed for non-seasonal series (ADF, PP, KPSS) are not applied; stationarity is assessed through visual inspection, ACF/PACF, and differencing.

\subsection{Train/test split}

The last $h=24$ months (March 2024--February 2026) form the test set. All models are estimated on the training sample (January 2011--February 2024).

\section{Decomposition methods}

\subsection{Multiplicative decomposition}

Because seasonal effects are more naturally expressed as proportional to the level when a strong trend is present, \textbf{multiplicative} decomposition is used:
\[
y_t = T_t \times S_t \times R_t,
\]
where $T_t$ is trend-cycle, $S_t$ seasonal, and $R_t$ remainder.

Figure~\ref{fig:decomp} shows the classical multiplicative decomposition. Seasonal factors fluctuate around 1.0 with a narrow range (roughly 0.98--1.05), confirming that seasonality is small in relative terms but non-negligible in theory.

STL is applied to $\log(y_t)$ so that additive components on the log scale correspond to multiplicative structure on the original scale (Figure~\ref{fig:stl}). The seasonally adjusted series is obtained as $y_t^{\mathrm{SA}} = y_t / S_t$ (Figure~\ref{fig:sa}).

\section{Smoothing methods}

\subsection{Holt-Winters (multiplicative)}

Holt-Winters with \textbf{multiplicative seasonality} is fitted to the training sample. Estimated smoothing parameters are $\alpha = 0.804$, $\beta = 0.018$, and $\gamma = 1.000$. Seasonal factors $s_m$ range from about 0.983 (February) to 1.047 (October). A damped-trend variant is also considered.

\subsection{ETS and STLF}

The \texttt{ets()} function selects \textbf{ETS(A,A,N)} on the training window (additive trend, no seasonal term; AIC $= 1981.5$). STLF combines STL with ETS forecasting on the seasonally adjusted component.

\section{ARIMA modelling}

\subsection{Transformations and differencing}

Prices grow over time and variance increases with the level. A \textbf{log transformation} stabilises variance; \textbf{first differencing} of $\log(y_t)$ removes the trend and yields a series that is approximately stationary (Figures~\ref{fig:log}--\ref{fig:difflog}). The ACF/PACF of the differenced log series (Figure~\ref{fig:acfdiff}) guide model identification.

\subsection{Model short-list and AIC}

Three candidates are compared on the training sample (all with $d=1$ on the log scale):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Candidate A:} \texttt{auto.arima} with \texttt{seasonal = FALSE}
  \item \textbf{Candidate B:} \texttt{auto.arima} with \texttt{seasonal = TRUE}
  \item \textbf{Candidate C:} manual SARIMA$(3,1,0)(0,0,1)_{12}$
\end{itemize}

Both auto-selected models yield \textbf{ARIMA$(1,1,4)$} (AIC $= -710.4$); the manual SARIMA has higher AIC ($= -693.4$). ARIMA$(1,1,4)$ is retained for diagnostics and forecasting. Forecasts on the original scale are obtained by exponentiating the log-scale predictions.

\subsection{Diagnostic checking}

Figure~\ref{fig:resid} shows residual diagnostics. The Ljung-Box test on innovation residuals at lag 24 with \texttt{fitdf} $= 5$ (estimated AR/MA coefficients) gives $Q^* = 16.80$, df $= 19$, $p = 0.60$, so residual autocorrelation is not significant at conventional levels.

\section{Forecast comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} summarises test-set accuracy. \textbf{ETS} achieves the lowest RMSE (326~EUR/m$^2$) and MAPE (9.6\%). Holt-Winters (multiplicative) and STLF perform similarly (RMSE $\approx$ 336). The damped Holt-Winters variant and the log-ARIMA model perform worse on this 24-month test window, with ARIMA showing the largest systematic under-forecast (MAPE 18.3\%).

All methods produce positive mean error: actual prices in March 2024--February 2026 rise faster than any model extrapolates from the training history. The 95\% intervals generally remain wide enough to contain much of the test path.

\section{Benefits and limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Multiplicative decomposition} respects proportional seasonality when the level trends strongly; for this series the seasonal effect is small but the choice is theoretically appropriate.
  \item \textbf{Holt-Winters (multiplicative)} captures trend and seasonality directly; performance is competitive though not best on the test set.
  \item \textbf{ETS} provides automatic model selection and achieves the lowest test RMSE here, but drops explicit seasonality on the training window.
  \item \textbf{STLF} combines robust STL with ETS; results are similar to Holt-Winters.
  \item \textbf{Log-ARIMA$(1,1,4)$} follows standard stationarity practice (log + differencing) and passes residual diagnostics, but under-performs on the test set when growth accelerates; back-transformed intervals assume log-normal innovations.
  \item \textbf{General limitation:} all methods assume historical patterns continue; none fully captures the 2024--2026 acceleration in prices.
\end{itemize}
